About the workshop

From accurate models to clinically trustworthy ones

Neurological and cardiac disorders are among the leading causes of death and disability worldwide, yet many remain hard to diagnose early, monitor over time, and treat effectively.

TrustMedAI brings the biomedical imaging community together with neurologists, cardiologists, neuroradiologists, interventional radiologists, neurosurgeons, cardiac surgeons, and imaging scientists. Our focus is AI that is multimodal, robust across institutions and populations, interpretable, uncertainty-aware, fair, and ready for high-stakes clinical use.

We give special attention to areas that have received comparatively little attention from the AI community — stroke and cerebrovascular disease on the neurological side, and heart failure, arrhythmia, and structural heart disease on the cardiac side — and we treat datasets and benchmarks as first-class contributions.

Modalities

MRI, CT, PET, ultrasound & echocardiography, angiography (DSA, MRA, CTA, coronary), digital pathology, and video-based assessment.

Fused with

Biosignals (EEG, ECG), genomics, laboratory data, EHRs and clinical notes — via vision-language, foundation, and world models.

Imaging in focus

Angiography meets trustworthy AI

Vascular imaging is central to both disease domains. We want models that find what matters on MR and CT angiography and also say how sure they are.

Six main directions

01

Multimodal AI

Fusion of imaging, signals, genomics and records for neurological and cardiac disorders.

02

Neurosymbolic & knowledge-guided AI

Combining learning with guidelines, anatomical and physiological constraints, and causal reasoning.

03

Trustworthy & explainable AI

Uncertainty quantification, calibration, interpretability, fairness and bias auditing.

04

Diagnosis-to-outcomes continuum

AI that follows the patient from detection through treatment to recovery.

05

Datasets, benchmarks & generalization

New cohorts, annotation protocols, and evaluation across institutions and populations.

06

Translation & human–AI collaboration

Verification, validation, regulatory readiness, deployment and trust calibration.

Call for papers

What we're looking for

We welcome work that bridges methodological advances with clinically meaningful problems, and that evaluates models beyond accuracy — by calibration, generalization, fairness, robustness, interpretability, and deployment readiness.

Neurological focus

  • Stroke and cerebrovascular disease (aneurysm, ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke)
  • Neurodegenerative disorders and dementia
  • Epilepsy
  • Brain tumors and neuro-oncology
  • Other neurological conditions

Cardiac focus

  • Heart failure
  • Arrhythmia and electrophysiological interventions
  • Structural heart disease and repair
  • Coronary artery disease

Methods

Multimodal fusion; foundation and vision-language models; domain adaptation; uncertainty; explainability; federated and privacy-preserving learning; robustness to distribution shift; causal, neurosymbolic and world-model approaches.

Datasets & benchmarks

Reviewed on equal footing with methods papers, judged on clinical relevance, data quality, documentation, annotation rigor, licensing and accessibility, reproducibility, and community reuse.
